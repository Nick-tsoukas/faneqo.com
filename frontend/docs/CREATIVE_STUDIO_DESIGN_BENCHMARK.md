# Faneqo Creative Studio — Music-Industry Design Benchmark

Written 2026-10-11. Web research for the Creative Studio design-quality sprint. Read with
`docs/CREATIVE_STUDIO_DESIGN_STANDARD.md`, which turns these observations into the standard and
scorecard used for every template refinement. What is actually on screen now is
`docs/CREATIVE_STUDIO_VISUAL_HANDOFF.md`. Use this file for principles. Do not copy the campaigns.

What this is: observed design qualities of real music-industry creatives, with source links.
What it is not: performance claims. Where a source reports measured results it is quoted and
attributed; everywhere else the entry says "none". Nothing here is to be copied; distinctive
artwork, lettering, and layouts belong to the artists and studios credited.

Method: ~24 search / fetch calls. Entries marked "as described by" rely on the source's text
rather than an image we could inspect.

## 1. References

### Spotify for Artists — Marquee, Showcase, Canvas

**1. Magic City Hippies, *Water Your Garden* (Marquee + Canvas sequencing)**
[Case study](https://artists.spotify.com/blog/how-magic-city-hippies-sequenced-marquee-canvas-and-discovery-mode) ·
[Format](https://artists.spotify.com/display-campaigns) · [Canvas guidelines](https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/canvas-guidelines/)
- Dominant element: the release artwork at full fidelity in a full-screen modal; Canvas adds an 8-second 9:16 loop built from B-roll.
- Type: platform-controlled. One short contextual headline from a fixed set ("Releasing music soon", "On tour").
- Branding: Spotify chrome frames; the artwork is the only brand expression.
- CTA: one tap, one destination.
- Lesson: artwork + one contextual line + one action, sequenced over months. Canvas's rule "don't repeat what the surface already shows" is a discipline for any template.
- Performance (reported by Spotify): Marquee viewers "four times more likely to stream the singles"; display campaigns "over 3.7x more likely to stream the promoted release". Platform-reported, not independently verified.

### Apple Music artist promotion

**2. Apple Music artist billboards (2015 global OOH)**
[AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/15/10/16/following-shot-on-iphone-6-campaign-new-billboards-featuring-apple-watch-apple-music-target-global-audiences)
- As described: full-bleed artist portrait with "Apple Music" set in a display typeface chosen per artist.
- Two tiers only: photograph, then one mark. No copy, no dates.
- Lesson: the purest Artist Spotlight structure. The artist's world sets the tone; the platform mark stays constant and small. Performance: none.

**3. Apple Music "Up Next: Class of 2017"**
[Andy Baker](https://www.andy-baker.com/apple-music-up-next-class-of-2017) · [Program](https://artists.apple.com/support/1123-up-next-has-gone-global)
- As described: eight artists rendered in one owned visual device (stop-motion-style 3D figures).
- Lesson: a platform can spotlight many artists with one consistent treatment without flattening them. Performance: none.

### Live Nation announcements

**4. Lil Baby, WHAM World Tour admat set; Bad Bunny tour announcement**
[Bad Bunny newsroom page](https://newsroom.livenation.com/news/bad-bunny-sets-out-for-global-domination-with-the-long-awaited-announcement-of-his-debi-tirar-mas-fotos-world-tour/)
- Confirmed from the asset list: every announcement ships as a ratio family — 4:5 (2160×2700), 9:16 (2160×3840), 1:1 (2160×2160) — plus a separate press photo. The on-sale date and URL are a structured block.
- Lesson: design the ratio family, not the poster; keep the action block structured. Performance: none.

### Boiler Room

**5. *System Restart* digital festival identity (Nejc Prah, 2020–21)**
[Project](https://www.nejcprah.com/projects/system-restart/) · [Creative Review on Boiler Room's house style](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/boiler-room-hayden-martin-ai-photography-music/)
- Deliberate maximalism ("horror vacui"); each of four stages has its own typographic mood and colour wave; Stories assets built in the same system.
- Lesson: clutter is earned only when clutter is the message. The reusable idea is "palette and type mood rotate per segment". Performance: none.

### Festivals

**6. Coachella lineup poster system**
[Festival Insider](https://festival-insider.com/coachella-lineup-posters-every-one-since-1999/) · [Fonts In Use](https://fontsinuse.com/typefaces/151003/coachella)
- Size-graded stacked type; fans "parse the font size". Weak at the small tiers on a phone.
- Lesson: type size is a credible status signal; the platform mark never sits in the top tier. Performance: none.

**7. Glastonbury 2025, Stanley Donwood**
[Wallpaper*](https://www.wallpaper.com/art/music/stanley-donwood-interview-glastonbury-poster-design)
- As described: stained-glass motif, woodblock-style lineup type; one artistic voice over two decades.
- Lesson: consistency of author can be a brand. Performance: none.

**8. Roskilde RF25, e-Types / Playtype**
[Fonts In Use](https://www.fontsinuse.com/uses/73622/roskilde-festival) · [Playtype](https://playtype.com/commission/roskilde/)
- One owned orange, one owned typeface (with a condensed cut added for narrow formats), one geometric device; layout structure kept year to year.
- Lesson: that is the right size of platform branding for a social creative. Performance: none.

**9. Primavera Sound, Mucho**
[Mucho](https://wearemucho.com/work/primavera-sound) · [THRU motion](https://thrumotion.com/primavera-sound/)
- A constructive type system with 50+ weights, each standing for a musical style; "homogeneity is not an option".
- Lesson: one system can hold many genres without imposing one look. Performance: none.

### Artist release campaigns and independent tour posters

**10. Tyler, the Creator, *Chromakopia***
[Fonts In Use](https://www.fontsinuse.com/uses/64159/tyler-the-creator-chromakopia-album-art-and-c)
- One display face (modified), one text face, one filter (sepia), one accent colour, one motif — identical on cover, tour poster, trucks, site.
- Lesson: a release world needs very few elements, applied everywhere. Performance: none.

**11. Gold Celeste, *The Glow* anniversary tour poster (Thirst & Co); Alice Phoebe Lou EU 2025; Myriam Gendron Europe tour**
[Gold Celeste](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/68917/gold-celeste-and-vinyl-williams-the-glow-10th) ·
[Alice Phoebe Lou](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/70088/alice-phoebe-lou-eu-tour-2025-poster) ·
[Myriam Gendron](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/68222/myriam-gendron-europe-tour-poster)
- The indie standard: one characterful face for the artist name, one plain face for dates and logistics.
- Lesson: hard-code that pairing in a template — expressive name slot, strictly legible CTA / date slot. Performance: none.

### Record-label campaigns

**12. Fontaines D.C., *Skinty Fia* (Partisan)**
[Case](https://musically.designfoody.com/fontaines-dc/) · [Creative Commission](https://creative-commission.com/project/fontaines-dc/partisan-records/artwork/outlierdesign) ·
contrast with [*Romance* (XL)](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/fontaines-dc-romance-album-visuals/)
- One unrepeatable red-lit photograph reused on billboard, Canvas, Apple motion artwork, and ads; hand-painted lettering as the brand. The later *Romance* campaign changed everything except the typeface.
- Lesson: one hero image strong enough for 1:1, 4:5, and 9:16 without cropping the subject out; keep the permanent element permanent. Performance: none for the design itself.

**13. LoneLady, *Former Things* (Warp, Instruct Studio)**
[Instruct](https://instruct.studio/projects/lonelady/)
- Portraits layered with letraset, photocopy, and scalpel textures that became a typeface; the material system extends to singles, merch, stage.
- Lesson: texture reads as identity even at thumbnail size. Performance: none.

### Music creator software

**14. Splice, "Start with Sound" (Instrument)**
[Campaign](https://www.instrument.com/work/splice-campaign) · [Rebrand](https://www.instrument.com/work/splice) · see also [Ableton Live 12 artist shorts](https://www.raysume.com/live-12-artists/)
- An ownable photo treatment that behaves like the product (chop / echo); Ableton deliberately left its crisp look for a "dirty frame" artist-led style.
- Lesson: software for musicians sells by behaving like a music brand, not a tech brand. Performance: none.

**15. Linktree rebrand (Collins, 2022; evolved in-house)**
[Linktree blog](https://linktr.ee/blog/linktree-had-a-makeover) · [Behance](https://www.behance.net/gallery/146097989/Linktree) · [Aron Mayo](https://www.aronmayo.com/linktree)
- Creator photo in the hero as a cut-out silhouette; one green, one typeface; later added a condensed display face specifically for mobile and social legibility.
- Lesson: the closest category reference. Budget for mobile legibility from day one; let the brand dial down so the creator leads. Performance: none (audience-scale figures are not campaign metrics).

## 2. Recurring principles (strongest ten)

1. **One hero image, reused across every surface.** Skinty Fia; Apple billboards; Chromakopia.
2. **Two-tier type: expressive for the name, neutral for logistics and CTA.** Gold Celeste; Alice Phoebe Lou; Chromakopia.
3. **Type size encodes importance; the platform mark never sits in the top tier.** Coachella; Apple billboards; Marquee.
4. **One owned colour + one owned typeface is enough to be recognised for years.** Roskilde; Linktree; Chromakopia.
5. **Do not repeat what the surface already shows.** Canvas guidelines; Marquee's fixed headline set.
6. **Design the ratio family, not the poster.** Live Nation admats; Skinty Fia across Canvas / motion / Reels; Boiler Room Stories.
7. **Material texture is identity at thumbnail size.** LoneLady; Glastonbury; Splice.
8. **Flexible systems let many artists coexist without one imposed look.** Primavera; Up Next; Linktree's "dial up and down".
9. **Sequence creatives over a campaign arc.** Magic City Hippies; Skinty Fia; Chromakopia.
10. **Keep the permanent element permanent when everything else changes.** Fontaines D.C.; Roskilde; Glastonbury.

## 3. Anti-patterns

1. Press-release-first announcements: a wall of dates with the artwork as a download link.
2. Lineup density that needs zooming on a phone.
3. Template chrome overpowering the artist (the pre-2022 Linktree critique).
4. Maximalism without a reason.
5. Repeating on-screen metadata inside the creative.

## 4. Recommended Faneqo visual identity for creatives

Drawn from the above and the existing visual language (`docs/design/FANEQO_VISUAL_LANGUAGE.md`):

- **Frame, not subject.** Faneqo appears as the close (wordmark + destination) and at most one editorial label above it. Never a kicker, badge, wordmark, and URL all at once.
- **One type family, two voices.** Poppins stays. The artist name is the expressive tier: heavy, tight, uppercase, length-scaled. Labels and CTAs are the neutral tier: tracked caps, never below ~16px on a 1080 board, CTA ≥ 22px.
- **One accent system.** The existing magenta → violet → cyan gradient is the only colour that is "ours", used once per board (the accent word). Everything else is photograph, white, and near-black.
- **Photograph-led, format-specific.** Every family ships 1:1, 4:5, and 9:16 as separate art direction. Story frames fill the room with the photograph (blurred) rather than black.
- **Shade only where type lives.** Long photographic ramps; the top of the image stays open. Three treatment presets (Natural / Balanced / Deep) absorb photography differences instead of per-creative gradient editing.
- **Fixed contextual language.** Headline presets ("Artist Spotlight", "Meet {artist}", "Now on Faneqo") play the role of Marquee's fixed headline set; they are editorial labels, not mastheads.
- **Distinct family identities.** Spotlight = magazine profile; Song = release campaign; Product = the page, truthfully; Statement = manifesto; Education = calm editorial; Show = poster / ticket.

## 5. Most relevant to Artist Spotlight

Apple Music billboards (#2) for structure; Spotify Marquee (#1) for the one-line / one-tap
discipline; indie tour posters (#11) for the two-tier type rule; Skinty Fia (#12) for a hero
photograph that survives all three ratios; Linktree (#15) and Roskilde (#8) for the branding
budget; Canvas guidelines for what not to put in the frame.
